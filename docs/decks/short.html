Hi, I’m Vamshi Kanderao. First, what Déjà Vu is not: it’s not another chatbot. It’s an infer-then-confirm, user-owned, bounded memory layer for everyday personal agents. It’s a memory layer that any personal agent can add, Muse, ChatGPT, Claude, or your own, so it remembers you the way a good assistant would. It learns short cards from your chats, uses only the ones that fit each request, checks with you once when a guess really matters, and lets short-term things go on their own date. Confirmed memory for any agent. The chat you’ll see in the demo is just a host to show it.

02 / 06

Every personal agent today learns about you from your chats. That’s great, until something changes. You can’t see most of what it inferred. A guess from a chat two years ago carries the same weight as what you said yesterday, so it books the hotel you stopped liking. And it keeps re-reading years of history to work out who you are. I checked Muse, ChatGPT and Claude: all of them show you some memory, and none of them ever asks you to confirm it.

— The idea

Inferred

Confirmed

Uses every card

Guesses included. Confirming is never a gate.

Asks once

Only when a guess shapes a booking or plan.

Shows receipts

Every card links to the chat it came from.

03 / 06

So the idea is one line: inferred versus confirmed. Déjà Vu keeps learning and keeps using everything it learned, guesses included: confirming is never a gate in front of memory. What changes is that every card is marked as a guess or as confirmed, and when a guess is about to shape something that matters, like a booking, it asks once, in one line, and the answer still comes first. And every card shows where it came from. Let me show you.

05 / 06

Does it work? We tested three ways an agent can remember you, on fourteen people: two real chat histories and twelve synthetic people, 312 checks judged by Muse Spark, each counted once. Re-reading the full history got 70.5 percent right, a memory summary 71.8, and Déjà Vu memory 81.7. When life changed in ways that never came up in chat, Déjà Vu got 82.3 percent; the others, 49 and 48. It reads about 150 tokens of memory per message, against up to 39 thousand for the full history. Déjà Vu is best or tied for 13 of the 14 people; it loses on one. To be honest about the method: ten of the synthetic people were written after the engine was built, and the builder was tuned with two of their builds in view, so they are not a clean held-out set.
