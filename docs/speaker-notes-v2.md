# Déjà Vu: speaker notes for /narration-v2

## 1. Cover

Hi, I'm Vamshi Kanderao.

Déjà Vu isn't another chatbot. It's a memory layer that plugs into any personal agent you use. Muse, ChatGPT, Claude or your own.

It learns from your chats, checks when it matters, and today it runs on Meta's Muse models.

## 2. The problem → the idea

Here's the issue. Your agent remembers you, but never checks if it's still true.

You can't see what it guessed, or why.

So an old assumption either gets buried,

or gets too expensive to reach the agent.

Déjà Vu flips it: it runs on what you confirm.

Every card shows its source.

It asks once, only when it matters most.

And it reads short cards, not a summary.

No setup: just drop in your chat export.

## 3. Demo

Now let's watch a live demo to see how Déjà Vu makes you the driver, not the passenger, of your agent's memory.

## 4. Proof

This isn't a theory. I ran an eval on twelve personas and two people's real chats with Muse.

Déjà Vu got eighty percent right. A summary, seventy-two.

When something changed outside the chat: eighty-one percent; the others, about half.

And ninety-four percent fewer tokens.

## 5. The takeaway

Agents will keep getting better at guessing who you are.

But a guess is still a guess. Confirmed memory beats inferred memory.

## 6. Under the hood

Under the hood: Muse Spark writes the cards, Muse Wonder grades each one, memory stays bounded, and any agent plugs in over HTTP.

## 7. End

Next time an agent says it knows you, ask it to show you. Thank you.
